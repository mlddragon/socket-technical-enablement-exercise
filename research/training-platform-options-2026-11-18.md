# Training format options for the Socket exercise

Reviewed November 18, 2026. This is tool research, not a selection or authorization to start building. The exercise's planned teaching package is a lab of 30 minutes or less, a one-page customer talk track, and an observable readiness check ([project outline](../project/teaching-package.md)). Prices, limits, and sharing behavior can change; confirm again when creating the artifact.

## Recommendation

Build **one short, responsive learning experience** with three stops: (1) a scenario and goal, (2) a hands-on lab with evidence checkpoints, and (3) a readiness decision with feedback. Keep the one-page talk track as a printable companion. This lets the candidate demonstrate training design, not merely visual design.

**Best no-code combination:** Canva Free website for the learner-facing hub plus Tally Free for the readiness check. Canva supports free website publishing, but its free plan limits the my.canva.site domain to five published sites and published pages to ten sections each ([Canva Websites](https://www.canva.com/help/canva-websites/)). Tally supports unlimited free forms and responses, conditional logic, and free embedding; its own guide documents embedding in a Canva site ([Tally pricing](https://tally.so/pricing), [Canva embed guide](https://tally.so/help/how-to-embed-a-free-form-in-canva)). Make the lab use synthetic examples and sanitized screenshots; a hosted site should not expose private Socket account data.

**Best single-tool interactive option:** Genially Free, if a branching scenario is the centerpiece. It offers interactive presentations, quizzes, and scenario paths without coding ([plans](https://genially.com/plans/), [scenario builder](https://genially.com/create/scenario-based-learning/)). The material privacy limit is decisive: Genially says free creations are visible in search engines by default and private/restricted sharing is paid ([sharing help](https://help.genially.com/en_us/how-to-share-your-genially-creations-rkXUqDH2j)). Use it only for material safe to publish.

## Suggested deliverable shape

1. **Opening:** one-screen customer problem and the learning objective.
2. **Practice:** a short decision point asking whether the evidence indicates a policy action, package-manager/runtime failure, or uncertain result; show why each choice is right or premature.
3. **Lab:** five to seven steps that can be completed in 30 minutes or less, with safe evidence checkpoints.
4. **Readiness:** three to five scenario questions with explanation and a visible “ready / needs practice” rubric.
5. **Job aid:** a printable one-page talk track for customer questions and escalation cues.

The actual topic, examples, and product behavior should follow hands-on verification and the final teaching-package choice. No paid subscription, account creation, or product implementation is implied by this research.

# Socket Discord: enablement signals, May–November 2026

Reviewed 2026-11-15 in Mason's logged-in Socket Discord server. This is interview research, not a product defect log or a claim about current Socket behavior. It stays in `research/`; no lab result or training deliverable is recorded here.

## Scope and evidence rules

I reviewed the visible history in `#questions`, `#socket-cli`, `#firewall`, `#socket-dashboard`, `#feature-feedback`, and `#chat`, focusing on posts dated May 1–November 15, 2026. `#questions`, `#socket-cli`, and `#feature-feedback` are forums. The `#firewall` and `#socket-dashboard` forum views displayed no posts at review; relevant Firewall discussion was in `#questions` and `#chat`. The November `#chat` items I saw did not add relevant product friction. This is a bounded review of accessible Discord content, not a complete audit of support tickets, releases, documentation, or private follow-up.

- **Verified observation** means the message, date, link, and visible reply sequence were inspected in Discord. It does **not** mean the reported product behavior was independently reproduced.
- **Attributed user report** means a named community member described an experience or asked a question. Treat cause, scope, and current relevance as unverified.
- **Staff response** means a reply from `jess_ican` or Sarah Gooding. Their Discord profiles showed `moderator` and `admin` roles in this server; employment was not independently checked.
- **Status** describes only the public Discord thread as seen on November 15. “No public outcome” does not rule out private support, a later fix, or a documentation update.
- **Enablement idea** is an inference for interview preparation. Validate it against current official docs and a lab before teaching it as product behavior.

## Prioritized enablement opportunities

1. **P1 — Installation and integration troubleshooting path.** D-03, D-04, D-07, and D-11 span Docker/`uv`, Node version managers, `pnpm dlx`, TLS trust, and Deno package resolution. A short lab could ask a learner to identify the package manager and runtime path, compare wrapped and native commands, capture versions and certificates, and decide when to escalate. A supported-installation matrix should name prerequisites, known integration boundaries, and a recovery path for broken aliases. **Assumption to test:** these cases share enough diagnostic steps to make one reusable playbook. Do not teach a single root cause.
2. **P1 — Firewall operating model and plan/policy guide.** D-01, D-02, and D-05 show uncertainty about what `sfw` wraps, what a “no package fetch” message means, which proxy deployment is available, and which policy controls belong to each plan. Build a current-doc-checked one-page matrix and a demo showing a real package fetch, a command with no fetch, and how to distinguish policy action from tool/runtime error. **Assumption to test:** a clear “when Firewall intervenes” explanation will reduce setup confusion. The pricing and capability matrix needs current owner verification.
3. **P2 — Finding triage and customer-facing explanation.** D-08 is a useful resolved example: suspicious published artifacts were classified as an anomaly, and the reporter accepted that distinction. D-10 shows how a user may contest an AI-generated risk rationale. A readiness exercise could require evidence collection, calibrated language (“reported,” “anomaly,” “confirmed malicious”), and a support handoff without promising a classification. **Assumption to test:** a standard triage talk track helps staff preserve trust while avoiding premature conclusions.
4. **P2 — Visible support closure and documentation feedback.** D-06, D-09, D-10, and D-11 show unanswered or support-routed questions with no public technical outcome. The interview exercise could propose an acknowledgement template, issue owner and tracking cue, next-update expectation, and a check for whether the answer should become a doc correction. This is a workflow proposal, not a claim about Socket's private support performance.
5. **P3 — Ecosystem coverage and request routing.** D-09 and D-12 point to unclear expectations for Gerrit and Zed. A current support matrix should separate “supported,” “possible through a workaround,” and “requested,” then identify where feature feedback is logged. Do not infer support status from the Discord posts alone.

## Checks before turning this into training

- Read current Socket installation, Firewall, policy, pricing, and ecosystem documentation; compare against these dated reports. The user-reported documentation gaps above may already be fixed.
- Run a sanitized local lab for the chosen setup scenario. Record OS, package manager, runtime, Socket version, exact command, expected/actual output, and whether the behavior still reproduces.
- Ask a Socket owner to confirm plan capabilities, supported integrations, and official escalation paths before presenting a customer-facing answer.
- Keep any lab observations and training design in `project/` with references back to IDs D-01–D-12. Do not copy account details or credentials into this public repository.
